Level 2: Sector Level (Priority Mapping)
Objective: Identify which industrial sectors are most ready for Artificial Intelligence investment.
• The Risk/Reward Matrix: A 2x2 bubble chart visualising industrial sectors (e.g., Agriculture, Automotive, Healthcare, Energy).
• Axes Definition:
    ◦ X-Axis (Competitiveness): How strong the country is currently in that sector.
    ◦ Y-Axis (AI Penetration): How much that sector is being transformed by Artificial Intelligence globally.
• Quadrant Logic:
    ◦ High-Reward / Low-Risk (Top-Right): Sectors where the country is strong and Artificial Intelligence impact is high. These are the primary targets.
    ◦ High-Reward / High-Risk (Top-Left): Sectors with high Artificial Intelligence potential where the country currently lacks strength. These require capability building.


Level 3: Enterprise and Small and Medium-sized Enterprise Level (Operational Maturity)
refer to level3.docx in this folder.
Objective: To provide a granular technical and operational assessment of individual firms to determine their ability to extract value from Artificial Intelligence.
• Seven-Dimension Indicator System:
    1. Digital and Compute Infrastructure: Evaluates the availability of effective compute capacity relative to the workforce, the percentage of critical process lines with real-time telemetry, and the reliability of data platforms.
    2. Artificial Intelligence-Linked Data Assets: Measures the coverage of curated, versioned datasets for priority use cases, the efficiency of data labeling processes, and the use of centralized feature stores to ensure data reuse and consistency.
    3. Artificial Intelligence Engineering and Deployment: Assesses the level of Machine Learning Operations (MLOps) automation, the governance of model versions through registries, and the implementation of production monitoring for latency and drift.
    4. Innovation and Ecosystem: Measures Research and Development intensity as a share of sector value added, the output of patents and publications, and the rate at which pilot projects are successfully scaled to production.
    5. Human Capital and Workforce Capability: Evaluates workforce literacy through annual training, the depth of advanced technical roles (such as data engineers), and the enrolment of employees in reskilling programmes for roles impacted by automation.
    6. Artificial Intelligence Governance and Risk Management: Checks for an Artificial Intelligence Management System aligned with International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 42001, systematic risk assessments based on the National Institute of Standards and Technology framework, and compliance with data protection regulations.
    7. Value Realization and Impact: Quantifies the financial return on investment for deployments, measurable gains in operational productivity, and alignment with the Sustainable Development Goals, specifically focusing on environmental sustainability.
• Assessment Configurations:
    ◦ Enterprise Self-Assessment: A full 31-indicator assessment designed for large manufacturing firms, providing interactive dashboards with capability heatmaps and automated improvement recommendations.
    ◦ Small and Medium-sized Enterprise Rapid Assessment: A streamlined 10–20 indicator assessment focusing on foundational capabilities, designed as a low-cost entry point for firms in developing countries with limited resources.
• Maturity Level Mapping: Total scores are mapped to five levels: Foundational (0–20), Emerging (21–40), Scaling (41–60), Frontier (61–80), and Strategic Impact (81–100).

Level 4: Project Level (Lighthouse Feasibility)
refer to folder level4 in this folder.
Objective: To evaluate the readiness of specific technical cooperation or "lighthouse" pilot projects for successful deployment and long-term sustainability.
• The Two-Fold Evaluation Fold:
    1. Technical Readiness: Determines if the existing systems can capture, govern, and leverage data for Artificial Intelligence by examining Digital Infrastructure, Data Governance, and Artificial Intelligence/Machine Learning Readiness.
    2. Social Readiness: Assesses if people, policies, and institutions will support adoption by examining the Policy and Regulatory environment, Institutional Governance, and the User and Adoption landscape.
• Project Evaluation Lenses:
    ◦ Ethical Impact Assessment: Projects must be screened for potential ethical impacts throughout the technology life cycle, including the pre-design, development, testing, and operation stages.
    ◦ Data Readiness Assessment (Project Version): A diagnostic tool used to pinpoint gaps in national or sectoral data ecosystems and ensure that Artificial Intelligence adoption is aligned with national priorities.
• Lighthouse Performance Metrics:
    ◦ Efficiency Gains: Measurable outcomes such as cycle time reduction (e.g., the time delay between detecting a risk and the response) and cost reductions for similar services.
    ◦ Scale and Coverage: The total area or population covered by the service, such as the number of hectares of indigenous territories under monitoring by Artificial Intelligence-based fire management systems.
    ◦ Operational Stability: Percentage of system uptime for critical feeds and the average time required for human action following an Artificial Intelligence-generated alert.
• Implementation Phases:
    1. Garage Phase (Co-Design): Focused on discovery, design, and creating technical roadmaps or prototypes using collaborative methodologies.
    2. Development and Implementation Phase: Focused on building and piloting the solution and conducting formal measurements of its developmental impact.

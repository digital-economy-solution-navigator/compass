This Functional Requirement Document (FRD) outlines the necessary capabilities for the AI & Digital for Industry Navigator (AIDIN). This tool, developed through a collaboration between UNIDO and IBM with a framework designed by Oxford Insights, aims to measure a country's readiness to adopt and benefit from AI and digital technologies for industrial development .
1. Project Overview and Goals
The primary objective of AIDIN is to provide a comprehensive diagnostic tool that translates data on digital and AI readiness into actionable "Industrial Transformation Pathways" . Unlike standard indices that provide a "glamour list" of rankings, AIDIN is designed to be a one-stop shop for identifying industrial gaps and linking them to specific UNIDO solutions and partner interventions .
2. User Personas
The system must cater to three primary stakeholder groups:
• Government Analysts/Policy Advisors: Seeking data to justify investments and translate high-level strategies into operational actions .
• Industry Representatives: Focused on identifying solution providers, upskilling opportunities, and market expansion .
• Funding Partners/Development Ministers: Looking to de-risk investments and identify high-impact areas for funding .

3. Core Functional Requirements
3.1 Maturity Assessment Engine
The system must categorize countries into four maturity tiers rather than simple numerical rankings to avoid an overly competitive mindset :
1. Foundational: Lacks most enabling factors; very limited adoption .
2. Emerging: Some enabling factors present; early signs of adoption .
3. Scaling: Strong enabling factors, but limited capacity in outcome areas .
4. Frontier: Advanced ecosystems with widespread use and export capacity .
3.2 Pillar and Dimension Architecture
The platform must analyze and display data across nine core pillars (divided into enabling factors and outcomes) :
• Enabling Pillars:
    ◦ Infrastructure: Connectivity, Compute Capacity, and Energy .
    ◦ Data: Availability, Interoperability, and Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) .
    ◦ Innovation Ecosystem: Talent, Research Institutions, and Corporate market structure .
    ◦ Capital: Public and Private (Domestic vs. Foreign) .
    ◦ Governance: National Strategy, Regulatory Environment, and Enforcement .
• Outcome Pillars:
    ◦ Digital Adoption: Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS), Automation Maturity, and Digital Twins .
    ◦ AI Adoption: Operations Optimization and Smart Logistics .
    ◦ Digital Development: Servitisation and Digital Product Innovation .
    ◦ AI Development: AI Product Innovation .
3.3 Dashboard and Visualization
• National Snapshot: Provide a high-level view of a country's current digital and AI maturity .
• Radar/Bar Charts: Utilize visualizations (similar to the UNDP Digital Development Compass) to help countries pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses within their industrial ecosystems .
• Sectoral Deep-Dives: Where data permits, enable a view of specific industries (e.g., textiles, coffee, or automotive) rather than just an aggregated national "blob" .
• Benchmarking: Allow users to compare their country’s performance against peer groups based on region or income level .
3.4 Transformation Pathway Navigator (The "Solution Link")
• Automated Gap Analysis: The system must identify performance gaps in the assessment and intelligently tag relevant solutions from the UNIDO Solution Platform, Digital Economy Solution Navigator, and Aim Global marketplace .
• Bespoke Service Menu: Generate a curated menu of interventions (e.g., upskilling programs, center of excellence setups, or policy frameworks) adjusted to the country's specific maturity level .
• AI-Curated Content: Optionally use generative AI to analyze unstructured data (reports/case studies) to offer context-specific next steps for local stakeholders .
3.5 Data Management and Collection
• Multi-Source Integration: Capability to aggregate data from over 50 existing indices and external datasets .
• User-Submitted Evidence: Provide a portal for member states to submit evidence (e.g., new national strategies or budget allocations) to be verified and incorporated into the model .
• Data Portability: Allow secondary users (researchers/analysts) to download raw data for offline use .
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Non-Functional Requirements
• Accessibility: The framework must be legible to users regardless of their familiarity with data science or industrial policy .
• Mobile Responsiveness: Key leadership should be able to access "pocket intelligence" via smartphones during high-level negotiations .
• Privacy and Trust: Data collection must follow strict UN protocols; input materials used for fine-tuning should be stored temporarily and deleted immediately after processing .
• Agility: The tool must be capable of iterative learning, allowing it to improve as new deployment stories and technical standards emerge
